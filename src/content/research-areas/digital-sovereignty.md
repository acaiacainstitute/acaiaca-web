<section class="research-hero" aria-labelledby="sovereignty-title">
  <div class="page-shell research-hero-grid">
    <div>
      <p class="eyebrow">Research area</p>
      <h1 id="sovereignty-title">Digital Sovereignty</h1>
      <p class="research-hero-question">Who controls the persistent state that carries an AI-mediated relationship through time?</p>
      <p class="research-hero-orientation">Continuing AI-mediated relationships can depend on identity, authorization, memory, preferences, provenance, delegated state, representation, and institutional recognition—not merely stored data.</p>
      <p class="research-hero-orientation">The sovereignty question begins when control over the continuing relationship cannot be understood by looking at any one layer alone.</p>
    </div>
    <aside class="research-status" aria-label="Research maturity">
      <p class="research-status-label">Research maturity</p>
      <strong>Early active inquiry</strong>
      <p>Acaiaca is investigating whether these layers form a coupled governance problem. It is not asserting that sovereignty is a validated unitary property.</p>
    </aside>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-section" aria-labelledby="sovereignty-problem-title">
  <div class="page-shell research-section-grid">
    <header>
      <p class="eyebrow">Why this is a research problem</p>
      <h2 id="sovereignty-problem-title">The relationship can outlive the interaction.</h2>
    </header>
    <div class="research-copy">
      <p class="research-lede">A system may later rely on remembered context, identity, authorization, prior instructions, preferences, provenance, delegated workflows, service relationships, or institutional recognition.</p>
      <blockquote class="research-distinction">
        <p><strong>What persistent technical and institutional state carries the relationship forward?</strong></p>
        <p>Who controls it; which parts may continue, transfer, expire, or be revoked; and what must be recognized again when context changes?</p>
      </blockquote>
      <p class="research-note">Digital Sovereignty owns the persistence-and-control relationship—not privacy generally, IAM generally, practical agency generally, legitimate mandate generally, or responsibility allocation generally.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-section research-section--wash" aria-labelledby="sovereignty-relationships-title">
  <div class="page-shell">
    <div class="research-section-grid">
      <header>
        <p class="eyebrow">Candidate decomposition</p>
        <h2 id="sovereignty-relationships-title">Relationships under investigation</h2>
      </header>
      <p class="research-lede">Acaiaca is not assuming that digital sovereignty is one indivisible property. The visible decomposition is provisional and must not be read as a final ontology.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="sovereignty-relationship-list" aria-label="Candidate relationships under investigation">
      <article><h3>Identity &amp; authentication</h3><p>What person, role, account, agent, or relationship is continuing—and what technical identity claim has actually been verified?</p></article>
      <article><h3>Authorization</h3><p>What operations does the system currently permit, and when should continuing permission itself become suspect?</p></article>
      <article><h3>Memory / context</h3><p>What history, instructions, assumptions, workflow state, or learned patterns persist?</p></article>
      <article><h3>Preferences</h3><p>Remembered preference is not the same thing as continuing mandate or authority.</p></article>
      <article><h3>Representation &amp; delegation state</h3><p>Whom or what is the system acting for, and what delegated relationship is being carried forward?</p></article>
      <article><h3>Provenance</h3><p>Where did persistent state come from, how was it transformed, and under what authority was it introduced?</p></article>
      <article><h3>Portability, revocation &amp; expiry</h3><p>What can move while preserving meaning, what can be terminated, and what should stop carrying forward unless renewed?</p></article>
      <article><h3>Institutional recognition</h3><p>What state does another institution accept as valid, and what must be re-established across providers or organizations?</p></article>
    </div>

<section class="research-section" aria-labelledby="sovereignty-distinctions-title">
  <div class="page-shell research-section-grid">
    <header>
      <p class="eyebrow">Important distinctions</p>
      <h2 id="sovereignty-distinctions-title">Technical continuity is not institutional validity.</h2>
    </header>
    <div class="research-copy sovereignty-distinctions">
      <blockquote class="research-distinction"><p><strong>Privacy is necessary context, not the whole sovereignty problem.</strong></p><p>Privacy asks whether information flows and uses are appropriate. Digital Sovereignty asks who controls persistent state and relationships over time.</p></blockquote>
      <blockquote class="research-distinction"><p><strong>Authentication is not legitimate authority.</strong></p><p>Authentication can establish that a technical identity or credential claim is valid; it does not establish mandate or institutional standing.</p></blockquote>
      <blockquote class="research-distinction"><p><strong>Portability is not revocation.</strong></p><p>Being able to move state is not the same as being able to end the old relationship.</p></blockquote>
      <p class="sovereignty-mode-note">A technical mechanism may support continuity without establishing that the continuity is institutionally recognized, legitimately authorized, normatively permissible, or legally entitled.</p>
      <p class="research-note sovereignty-lineage">Privacy, identity, authorization, portability, provenance, delegation, interoperability, and institutional trust already explain important parts of the problem. Acaiaca’s question is whether persistent AI-mediated relationships create a coupled governance problem when those mechanisms must remain coherent across time, providers, and institutions.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

  <div class="page-shell research-section-grid">
    <header>
      <p class="eyebrow">Provisional research object</p>
      <h2 id="persistent-state-title">Persistent Agentic State</h2>
    </header>
    <div class="research-copy">
      <p class="research-lede">Acaiaca uses Persistent Agentic State as a provisional way of asking what technical and institutional state must persist for an AI-mediated relationship to continue functioning across interactions, systems, providers, or organizations.</p>
      <p class="sovereignty-object-status">Provisional layered construct · parent-owned</p>
      <p class="research-note">The research is not assuming that every layer should persist, transfer, or be governed the same way. This is not a validated model, production architecture, standard, ontology, or independent destination.</p>
    </div>

</section>

  <div class="page-shell">

      <header>
        <p class="eyebrow">Current work</p>
        <h2 id="sovereignty-current-title">Testing whether persistent state creates a joint sovereignty problem</h2>
      </header>
      <p class="research-lede">One coupled inquiry carries the current work. The proposition, provisional construct, and operationalization agenda occupy different analytical roles; they are not three projects or maturity stages.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="sovereignty-current-meta"><span>Research context: September 2026</span><span>One coupled inquiry · no empirical finding implied</span></div>
    <div class="sovereignty-coupled" aria-label="Coupled current-work inquiry">
      <article>
        <header><h3>P11 · Proposed</h3><p>Proposed integrative proposition</p><p><a href="/research/agenda/#p11">Read P11 and its Research Agenda context →</a></p></header>
        <p>Persistent AI systems may create sovereignty problems not fully addressed by conventional account ownership, application-level privacy controls, ordinary data portability, or isolated governance of individual state layers.</p>
      </article>
      <article>
        <header><h3>Persistent Agentic State</h3><p>Provisional layered construct · parent-owned</p></header>
        <p>Provides the current decomposition through which P11 is examined: which technical and institutional relationships actually carry continuity across time and boundaries?</p>
      </article>
      <article>
        <header><h3>Layer-specific operationalization</h3><p>Current analytical agenda</p></header>
        <p>Portability, revocation, expiry, provenance, authorization continuity, institutional recognition, and cross-provider continuity are examined without assuming one universal rule across every layer.</p>
      </article>
    </div>
    <p class="sovereignty-falsifiability">If established privacy, identity, authorization, portability, provenance, delegation, and institutional mechanisms prove sufficient when appropriately integrated, the stronger sovereignty claim should be narrowed or rejected.</p>
  </div>
</section>

  <div class="page-shell">
    <div class="research-section-grid">
      <header>
        <p class="eyebrow">Applied inquiry</p>
        <h2 id="sovereignty-transition-title">A transition exposes the seams.</h2>
      </header>
      <div class="research-copy">
        <p class="research-lede">Imagine a person who has used an AI assistant inside an employer over time and then changes employer and provider. The question is not “can all their state move?”</p>
        <p class="research-note">Illustrative transition scenario—not validation or a reference architecture.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="sovereignty-open-grid">
      <article><h3>What may move?</h3><p>Which personal preferences or context can transfer while preserving meaning and provenance?</p></article>
      <article><h3>What must expire?</h3><p>Which permissions, delegated tasks, or institutional recognitions end with the old role?</p></article>
      <article><h3>What must be re-established?</h3><p>Which identity, mandate, authorization, or recognition cannot simply migrate?</p></article>
      <article><h3>Did the old relationship actually end?</h3><p>Can the prior system still act, retain live permissions, or continue using stale relationship state?</p></article>
    </div>
