<section class="research-hero" aria-labelledby="agency-title">
  <div class="page-shell research-hero-grid">
    <div>
      <p class="eyebrow">Research area</p>
      <h1 id="agency-title">Human–AI Agency</h1>
      <p class="research-hero-question">What practical ability does a person retain when AI participates more deeply in activity on or for that person’s behalf?</p>
      <p class="research-hero-orientation">A system can become more capable without making the person more able to understand, influence, redirect, challenge, revoke, or exit what happens. It can also open possibilities the person did not previously have.</p>
      <p class="research-hero-orientation">System capability and human agency are different properties. Acaiaca investigates how their relationship changes with the task, the surrounding system, the person’s circumstances, and repeated use.</p>
    </div>
    <aside class="research-status" aria-label="Research maturity">
      <p class="research-status-label">Research maturity</p>
      <strong>Active inquiry</strong>
      <p>Emerging research area. Here, practical agency is a provisional working lens—not a validated scale or a complete theory of agency.</p>
    </aside>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-section" aria-labelledby="capability-title">
  <div class="page-shell research-section-grid">
    <header>
      <p class="eyebrow">Why this is a research problem</p>
      <h2 id="capability-title">Capability does not settle agency.</h2>
    </header>
    <div class="research-copy">
      <p class="research-lede">An AI-mediated workflow may handle recommendations, selection, coordination, remembered context, tools, or continuing actions. That arrangement can increase access, reduce cognitive burden, expose options, or enable action. It may also leave the person with less relevant understanding, less timely intervention, fewer feasible alternatives, or greater dependence. These are possibilities to examine, not universal effects.</p>
      <blockquote class="research-distinction">
        <p><strong>Human participation is not the same thing as human agency.</strong></p>
        <p>A person might approve a recommendation without enough context, observe a process without intervention rights, confirm an outcome with no realistic alternative, or receive an explanation after the useful intervention window has passed. An approval step may support agency very well. Its existence alone does not prove that it does.</p>
      </blockquote>
      <p>A control is an available mechanism. Practical agency concerns whether the person can meaningfully use it in the activity. Pause, cancel, edit, reset, override, and revoke may support agency, but their effect depends on accessibility, information, timing, consequences, feasible alternatives, and the surrounding institution.</p>
      <p>Practical agency can also change through outputs people rely upon, even when no tool executes an operation. Autonomous execution is not a prerequisite to an agency question.</p>
      <p class="research-note">Research on human interaction with automation has long examined the difference between successful system performance and people’s understanding, monitoring, and ability to intervene. Acaiaca inherits that problem; it does not claim to have discovered it.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-section research-section--wash" aria-labelledby="lens-title">
  <div class="page-shell">
    <div class="research-section-grid">
      <header>
        <p class="eyebrow">A provisional working lens</p>
        <h2 id="lens-title">What can the person meaningfully do?</h2>
      </header>
      <div class="research-copy">
        <p class="research-lede">Acaiaca uses practical human agency as a provisional way of examining a person’s real ability to understand what is happening, shape its course, intervene or redirect, exercise available revocation, challenge an outcome or process, and retain a realistic ability to exit or act otherwise.</p>
        <p>These questions make the current working lens concrete. They are not a validated scale, an exhaustive list, six independent variables, or six requirements that apply identically in every situation. Relevant abilities depend on the person’s role, the activity, timing, and surrounding conditions.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <ol class="agency-question-stack">
      <li><span class="agency-question-index" aria-hidden="true">01</span><div><h3>Can the person understand enough to act meaningfully?</h3><p>Do they know that AI is participating, what role it has, what information or assumptions matter, and what consequences may follow? They do not need to explain the model’s internals. They need enough relevant understanding to evaluate, shape, or intervene in this activity.</p><p class="agency-question-concept">Epistemic access / understanding</p></div></li>
      <li><span class="agency-question-index" aria-hidden="true">02</span><div><h3>Can the person shape what matters?</h3><p>Can they meaningfully affect the objectives, inputs, constraints, options, or course of the activity? A system may adapt wording or recommendations to preferences while leaving consequential choices outside the person’s influence.</p><p class="agency-question-concept">Influence / redirection</p></div></li>
      <li><span class="agency-question-index" aria-hidden="true">03</span><div><h3>Can the person intervene while it still matters?</h3><p>Can they pause, correct, redirect, override, stop, or recover the activity at a useful point? The opportunity must exist before relevant consequences become unavoidable—or offer meaningful recovery afterward.</p><p class="agency-question-concept">Intervention / override</p></div></li>
      <li><span class="agency-question-index" aria-hidden="true">04</span><div><h3>Can the person withdraw an authorization they have standing to withdraw?</h3><p>Initial permission does not establish an effective way to stop continuing activity. Where the person has revocation authority, can they exercise it and bring relevant future activity to an end?</p><p class="agency-question-concept">Revocation / delegation control</p></div></li>
      <li><span class="agency-question-index" aria-hidden="true">05</span><div><h3>Can the person challenge what happened through a consequential review path?</h3><p>Can they identify an error, dispute an outcome, request reconsideration, or seek review or redress? An explanation may help, but explanation alone is not a challenge process.</p><p class="agency-question-concept">Contestability / redress</p></div></li>
      <li><span class="agency-question-index" aria-hidden="true">06</span><div><h3>Can the person realistically leave, switch, or act otherwise?</h3><p>Stopping use is not the same as having a workable alternative. Consider available channels, switching costs, workflow dependence, retained access to needed information, portability, and the ability to continue through another arrangement.</p><p class="agency-question-concept">Realistic exit / substitutability</p></div></li>
    </ol>
    <aside class="agency-example" aria-labelledby="example-title">
      <h3 id="example-title">An illustrative work-planning situation</h3>
      <p>An employee uses an AI assistant to draft a weekly plan from team tasks. The assistant highlights conflicts the employee had missed, suggests priorities, and asks for confirmation before posting the plan.</p>
      <p>The assistance may expand what the employee can accomplish. But the confirmation step alone does not tell us whether they can inspect important omissions, change the priorities, or stop a mistaken update before colleagues rely on it. Editing wording is not the same as changing a consequential choice.</p>
      <p>Suppose the assistant can keep updating the plan. Can the employee withdraw that continuing authorization? Can they challenge an error through a review path, or continue planning through a workable alternative? After repeated use, their skill and dependence may change. Whether those changes expand or constrain practical agency remains a question.</p>
      <p class="research-note">This situation is pedagogical—not an Acaiaca case study or evidence. The employee’s practical ability also does not settle their authority over team commitments.</p>
    </aside>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-section" aria-labelledby="time-title">
  <div class="page-shell">
    <div class="research-section-grid">
      <header><p class="eyebrow">Longitudinal question</p><h2 id="time-title">What changes with repeated use?</h2></header>
      <p class="research-lede">Practical agency is not only an episode-level question. Assistance may alter competence, reliance, calibration, preferences, and feasible alternatives over time. These changes remain distinct; they do not reduce to one “agency score.”</p>
    </div>
    <div class="agency-time-grid">
      <article><h3>Retained competence</h3><p>If assistance improves immediate performance but changes a person’s ability to reason or act without it, what happens to their practical options over time? Retained competence is related to the lens, but it is not a seventh agency dimension.</p></article>
      <article><h3>Dependence &amp; calibration</h3><p>Reliance can expand what a person is able to do. The question is what happens when reliance changes their ability to intervene, use alternatives, or recognize when closer inspection is needed.</p></article>
      <article><h3>Preference drift &amp; exit</h3><p>How might repeated mediation change preferences, expectations, or habitual delegation—and how should those changes affect interpretation of later choices and the practicality of acting otherwise?</p></article>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-section research-section--wash" aria-labelledby="frontier-title">
  <div class="page-shell agency-lineage">
    <div><p class="eyebrow">Lineage to frontier</p><h2 id="frontier-title">What remains to be understood?</h2><p class="research-note">Human factors, human interaction with automation, meaningful human control, function allocation, distributed cognition, sociotechnical systems, HCI, participation, contestability, and redress already explain much of this terrain. Trust calibration, skill retention, and realistic alternatives also have established research lineages.</p></div>
    <div class="agency-frontier"><p>Acaiaca’s question is whether considering several practical abilities together helps trace how AI-mediated activity changes a person’s position over time—without replacing the established concepts that describe each ability more precisely.</p><p class="research-note">The integrated lens may prove useful. It may also need to be narrowed, decomposed, or replaced where existing approaches already do the job.</p></div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-section research-section--current" aria-labelledby="current-work-title">
  <div class="page-shell">
    <div class="research-section-grid">
      <header><p class="eyebrow">Current work</p><h2 id="current-work-title">What Acaiaca is working on now</h2></header>
      <p class="research-lede">Two integrated inquiry units carry the current work. They are not separate projects for every proposition, model, dimension, or longitudinal issue.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="agency-current-meta"><span>Research context: September 2026</span><span>No empirical study implied</span></div>
    <article class="agency-work-unit">
      <header><h3>Improving judgment-related performance while preserving agency</h3><p class="agency-object-meta">P8-centered joint-outcome inquiry · P8 Proposed · P6 Proposed supporting premise</p></header>
      <div class="agency-work-copy"><p>Acaiaca is examining judgment-related performance and practical agency as separate outcomes, asking which conditions could permit improvement in the first without material reduction in the second.</p><p><strong>Next movement:</strong> make the relevant tasks, assistance arrangements, performance criteria, agency dimensions, and temporal conditions sufficiently explicit to support a discriminating evaluation.</p></div>
      <div class="agency-proposition-pair">
        <div><h4>P6 · Foundational / inherited premise</h4><p><strong>Proposed.</strong> A more capable system does not necessarily give the person more practical agency. P6 prevents an unsupported inference from capability to agency; it is not an independent project.</p></div>
        <div><h4>P8 · Open / integrative proposition</h4><p><strong>Proposed.</strong> Under identifiable conditions, AI assistance may improve judgment-related performance without materially reducing practical agency. Better task results alone do not establish preservation.</p></div>
      </div>
    </article>
    <article class="agency-work-unit">
      <header><h3>Representing practical agency over time</h3><p class="agency-object-meta">Active construct and measurement problem · provisional multidimensional lens</p></header>
      <div class="agency-work-copy"><p>Acaiaca is investigating whether practical agency can be represented usefully across distinct abilities and how those abilities change under repeated AI assistance.</p><p><strong>Next movement:</strong> distinguish objective capacities from subjective assessments, test whether the integrated umbrella adds value beyond established component constructs, and clarify how episode-level tracing can inform later questions.</p></div>
      <div class="agency-loop"><h4>Human Agency Loop · parent-owned working model</h4><p>Acaiaca is evaluating a provisional tracing model for examining a human–AI decision or action episode: <strong>Perception ↔ Intent ↔ Judgment → Action → Consequence → Learning ↺</strong>. It asks who or what participates, what has been delegated, where practical intervention remains possible, and how consequences and feedback change the situation.</p><p>This is not a validated theory of cognition or a required chronology. Its additional diagnostic value remains unresolved, and no independent route is implied.</p></div>
    </article>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="research-section" aria-labelledby="open-title">
  <div class="page-shell">
    <div class="research-section-grid">
      <header><p class="eyebrow">Open questions</p><h2 id="open-title">What remains unresolved?</h2></header>
      <p class="research-lede">The work above identifies what Acaiaca is materially advancing in this area. The questions below identify what remains unresolved; they are not a list of separate active projects.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="agency-open-grid">
      <article><h3>Does the combined lens add anything?</h3><p>Can the proposed abilities be distinguished and evaluated reliably, and does treating them together reveal something that narrower established constructs do not?</p></article>
      <article><h3>What would preservation mean in this activity?</h3><p>Which practical abilities matter, are some minimum conditions, and would an overall score conceal a consequential loss?</p></article>
      <article><h3>What changes across time?</h3><p>How should practical capacities, subjective experience, independent competence, reliance, calibration, and feasible alternatives be examined together without collapsing them?</p></article>
      <article><h3>Whose agency is changing?</h3><p>When an activity spans people, systems, and continuing delegations, how can gains, losses, and redistribution be traced without treating one actor’s improvement as everyone’s improvement?</p></article>
    </div>
